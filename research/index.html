Research areas

My research develops mathematical programming formulations and algorithms for discrete, convex, and nonconvex optimization, with a particular emphasis on global optimization. The data available for a decision problem shape how I approach it. When data are plentiful, as in parametric problems solved repeatedly with changing inputs, I use machine learning to accelerate rigorous optimization while preserving its guarantees. When observations or operating conditions are uncertain, I use stochastic, robust, and adversarial models. This methodological core connects three application areas: power systems, networks and control, and quantum computing.

Across these areas, I focus on formulations and algorithms that retain physical fidelity and mathematical guarantees while remaining practical on realistic systems, especially critical infrastructure, where closely related problems must be re-solved as loads, forecasts, contingencies, and operating conditions change. I release these methods as open-source software so that others can use and build on them. In the diagram, each overlap marks where a methods contribution meets an application question.

Global Optimization

Global optimization provides the methodological core for solving difficult nonconvex and mixed-integer nonlinear models with rigorous bounds. This work develops adaptive partitioning, strong convex relaxations, and polyhedral and convex-hull formulations that focus computational effort where it most improves the global bound. The resulting algorithms support applications such as AC optimal power flow and transmission switching.

Learning-Accelerated Optimization

Many optimization problems are solved repeatedly as loads, forecasts, and other parameters change. This creates data that can be used to learn better search, partitioning, and bound-tightening strategies while mathematical optimization retains valid bounds and optimality guarantees. The work includes learning- augmented and reinforcement-learning-guided partitioning, accelerated tightening of AC optimal power flow relaxations, neural approximations for bilevel adversarial problems, and statistical models of electricity demand. These methods are developed alongside open-source optimization software, including Alpine.jl.

Area 2 of 6

Power Systems Optimization Under Uncertainty

Grid operating conditions are uncertain: renewables vary, equipment fails, and storms and attacks disrupt the system. To anticipate such conditions, this work develops chance-constrained and decomposition-based stochastic unit commitment and multi-stage stochastic optimal power flow under N-1 outages. To withstand worst-case events, it builds transmission switching and distributionally robust models for geomagnetic disturbances, robust renewable planning, bilevel attacker–defender models, and probabilistic N-k failure identification. Related work addresses electricity pricing, energy-storage arbitrage, and data-driven models of demand.

Resilient Power Transmission & Distribution Grids

Resilience spans both transmission and distribution systems. At the transmission level, this work develops optimization models for resilient grid and topology design, including comparisons of AC relaxations and DC approximations. At the distribution level, it addresses resilient feeder expansion and the design, partitioning, reconfiguration, operation, and black-start restoration of networked microgrids under uncertainty, with the objective of restoring and serving as much critical load as possible. The resulting software received a 2023 R&D 100 Award, was integrated into NRECA’s Open Modeling Framework, and was tested on a realistic Cobb EMC network.

Networks, Control & Robotics

This line of work, which began with the doctoral research on robust communication networks for unmanned aerial vehicles, uses spectral graph theory and mixed-integer optimization to design networks that are robust by construction. It includes formulations and algorithms for maximizing algebraic connectivity, topology design for disturbance minimization and stability in power grids and manufacturing networks, robustness in robot localization, and string stability of heterogeneous vehicle platoons.

Area 5 of 6

Quantum Computing

This work connects mathematical optimization and quantum computing in both directions. Mixed-integer programming enables provably optimal quantum circuit design, while hardware-aware encodings map constrained optimization problems efficiently to quantum devices. In the other direction, hybrid quantum–classical methods use quantum-generated candidate solutions within classical optimization algorithms, where classical subproblems evaluate candidates, enforce feasibility, and retain algorithmic guarantees. Related work benchmarks Ising processing units using integer programming.
