My research develops mathematical programming formulations and algorithms for discrete, convex, and nonconvex optimization, with a particular emphasis on global optimization. The data available for a decision problem shape how I approach it. When data are plentiful, as in parametric problems solved repeatedly with changing inputs, I use machine learning to accelerate rigorous optimization while preserving its guarantees. When observations or operating conditions are uncertain, I use stochastic, robust, and adversarial models. This methodological core connects three application areas: power systems, networks and control, and quantum computing.

Across these areas, I focus on formulations and algorithms that retain physical fidelity and mathematical guarantees while remaining practical on realistic systems, especially critical infrastructure, where closely related problems must be re-solved as loads, forecasts, contingencies, and operating conditions change. I release these methods as open-source software so that others can use and build on them. In the diagram, each overlap marks where a methods contribution meets an application question.
